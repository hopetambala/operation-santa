# Your team packet
{: .no_toc }

**Who:** Santa team leads. You don't need the app or a computer.
**When:** December 24, before you leave the North Pole.

1. TOC
{:toc}

{: .why }
In 2025 teams followed a route sheet from one tool and letters from another. Gate codes and apartment numbers were only on the letters, and letters had to be matched to stops by hand. Your packet now has everything in the order you'll drive it, and every bag says which stop it's for.

Your packet has five parts, in this order.

## 1. The cover

Check these before you leave:

- your **route** name and **team**, and the lead's phone number;
- the **North Pole** address and phone number: call it with any question;
- the number of **stops, children and bags**;
- **leave** and **back about** times.

The map shows your stops as numbered circles and the North Pole as **NP**. It isn't a driving map: use the QR codes.

<!-- screenshot: a demo route cover page with the QR codes -->

### Phone navigation

The QR codes at the bottom of the cover open your route in Google Maps, a few stops at a time.

1. Open your phone's camera and point it at **Leg 1**.
2. Tap the link that appears. Choose to open it in the **Google Maps app**, not the web browser: phone browsers show only a few stops.
3. When you reach the end of a leg, scan the next one.
4. The last leg ends back at the North Pole.

Your coordinator may also text you the same links. They contain addresses only, never names.

## 2. The route sheet

One row per stop, in order. Each row has:

- the estimated **arrival time** (an estimate; don't worry if you're a little early or late);
- the **family** name and Entry ID;
- the **address and unit**;
- the **gate code or access notes**;
- the family's **phone**;
- **children and bags**, for example "3 / 1";
- flags: **ES** someone at the house speaks Spanish, **+T** extra toys, **AF** adopt-a-family;
- a **box** to tick when you've delivered.

## 3. The packing manifest

Every bag, listed from your **last** stop to your **first**. Load the car in this order, ticking each bag, and your first stop's bags end up on top.

Each bag shows the stop, Entry ID, family, "Bag 1 of 2", and the children in it with their first choice.

## 4. The letters

One family after another, in stop order. Each family's first page has a green band saying "Route 3 · Stop 5 of 16". It shows the family's name, address, gate code and phone, a table of every child with their age and first and second choices, any notes, and then each child's letter and drawings.

"Rudolph Route" near the top is the committee's quiet name for one of the sign-up form's questions. You don't need to do anything with it.

## 5. The bag labels

One label per bag, matching the manifest: "Route 3 – Stop 5", Entry ID, "Bag 1 of 2", family name, and the children in that bag.

## If something's not right

- **"Letter held by coordinator. Ask the North Pole before delivering this family's gifts."** Call the North Pole before you go to the door.
- **Nobody's home.** Gifts aren't left at the door. Call the North Pole and tell them which stop you missed, then carry on.
- **Address or gate code doesn't work.** Call the family's number from the route sheet, then the North Pole.
- **You got replacement pages.** Throw away the old route sheet, manifest and labels. Keep the old letters for families who are still on your route, and use the new pages for everyone else.

## After your route

Head back to the North Pole. Every page of the packet says "Confidential family information. Shred after December 26.", so don't keep it or throw it in the recycling.
